2. Handjob ↔ member: what's actually there, and the three things missing
What exists is better than I expected: 7 shaft bones, ErectionToFlaccid on the body and a now-skinned shell (uncommitted) with sub-micron endpoints, verified topology correspondence, and a compliance knob (±5° on shaft1/shaft4/distributed) that applies from a captured erect base each frame.

The blockers:

The column isn't a column yet — it's two baked centerline arrays in a test component. GetContactFrame lerps build-time endpoint centerlines by Erection01 and returns center/tangent/radius only — no normal/binormal, so it can't even fill a GripFrame (which needs a reference normal for the palm plane). And their own characterization says why bone centers are not viable: at full erection the bone line matches the surface to 5.4 mm, but with the flaccid morph the surface drifts up to 150 mm from the bone chain (G8M_ARTIST_AUTHORED_ERECTION_MORPH_PROOF.md). A handjob at partial erection would have the hand gripping a phantom shaft. Fix: an editor-generated contact spine asset — per-erection-state station centre/radius sampled from the skinned mesh (the 33-station problem is already solved in PartnerRigAcceptanceBuilder.DeriveGeometryLine), interpolated at runtime across stations and states. Cheap, data-driven, and re-generated whenever a new morph is added. Then a thin PartnerAnatomy : IGripTarget evaluates one frame from it.
Compliance has no consumer. Nothing reads the grip. There's no load signal, no spring, no caps, no restore-to-authored outside the test component, and it's gated behind erect-only + shell-skinned (from your uncommitted change). The ingredient exists: TryGetProbe already reports per-probe RadialDistance/AllowedDistance/State, and GripStrength01 is on the controller. Publish a GripContactLoad (position, strength, aggregate penetration, per-digit contact count) from the controller; drive a PartnerCompliance component from it with a critically damped, base-relative, hard-capped offset (say ≤5–8°, ≤1 cm), applied after capture each frame, never accumulating. The design decision to make explicitly: the hand IK is upstream in the performer's Playable graph and solves against the unbent column. If the member yields on the same frame the hand is solved, the two fight and you get jitter/penetration. Use one-frame lagged feed-forward: member follows last frame's load, hand keeps its 3 mm pad clearance. That is the whole "compliance" architecture; don't cosolve.
Squeeze is not available from the current asset, full stop. Unity has exactly one shape per renderer, and DAZ's bone-ERC controls (Flacid, Cock Length, curves) demonstrably do not survive export — zero shape channels, zero animation curves, and bone-only reconstruction of the flaccid endpoint is 189 mm max / 28 mm RMS wrong (PLAYER_FLACCID_ERECT_ENDPOINT_COMPARISON.md). The good news: you have a working recipe for synthesizing shapes. Author two squeeze states in DAZ (soft/tight), export endpoints, and run the same body+shell delta solver to make Squeeze01/02 blendshapes. Drive weight from the load signal. That gets you real geometry deformation, on both surfaces, with no new runtime tech.
Two more member-side items you'll hit immediately: the donor pelvis follow (currently a LateUpdate position/rotation copy in the test component) must become production, and the male body has no home in the scene hierarchy. It's playernude — the player's body — but Player today is a camera rig, and hiding Genesis8Male.Shape hides the member too because body+graft are one welded mesh with no material-region split. If you want first-person framing, decide the presentation mode (FullBody / MemberOnly / GhostBody) and implement the region split before the 2-minute scene, or you'll be fighting the camera and clipping for a week.

3. Wardrobe: the guts are good, but nothing tells you what "layer" means — and layers have three homes
The conceptual gap is the UI's fault, not yours: a layer step is not a renderer toggle. Each distinct visible mask is a separately compiled body mesh (UV3 coverage-clipped), its own surface bindings, dissolve profile and footwear state. "Remove highest" lowers a ceiling through the populated layers. If everything is in Base (true today for second- and third-outfit — every assignment is layer: 0), one remove takes the whole outfit off and it feels like the control does nothing.

What I verified about "assignments don't take effect even after save" (your actual saved assets): config revision 2 holds first-outfit PeekaBoo=1, panty=2, generation 685f…; the compiled fit states are masks 6/2/0 with no issues; and the preset asset still says parts: layer 0 for both pieces. So the save did work — but into the configuration only. WardrobeGenerationPublisher.SnapshotPreset is the only code that writes layers back into WardrobePreset.Parts, and that runs at Publish, not Save. Runtime then has three sources of truth:

preset.Parts (stale — what the Inspector shows you),
configuration.Assignments (your edit — used only while ConfigurationApplies() is true),
compiled FitStates (mask-keyed, another window again).
And ConfigurationApplies() silently falls back to the stale preset parts whenever a released catalog's generation doesn't match the config's generation — after which every piece is Base again and your edit is invisible. There's also a copy bug: the window says "Base — Always the first layer removed" while RemoveHighestCeiling removes the highest layer first (Base last). That single sentence gives you the wrong mental model from the first click.

UI/workflow pass I'd do (guts untouched):

One source of truth. Save should call the same part writeback as publish; delete the config-vs-preset conditional or make a mismatch a loud error. Then the Inspector and the runtime can never disagree.
Make the ladder the interface, not the checkboxes. Replace the two toggle buttons with a single Base/1/2 selector, and render the actual undress order with piece names: Fully dressed → layer 2 off (panty) → layer 1 off (PeekaBoo) → naked. That is the only thing the artist needs to reason about.
Step-through preview at the current revision. Play the ladder in-scene using the existing OutfitAsync/TryRemoveLayerAsync + dissolve transitions. Ten seconds of feeling it will teach more than the mask numbers (Mask 6) ever will.
Copy fixes. Base = kept longest; 1 = middle; 2 = outer (removed first). Say that, and show the populated piece count per layer.
Say when a save forks. After a release, Save writes to Assets/Wardrobe/Candidates/working-<hash>/… — a different asset than the game scene references. That needs a warning banner, not silence.
Asset hygiene. Every assignment edit writes a whole new SetupCandidates/<assignment-hash> tree (body mesh, bindings, profile, fit state per mask). Key by revision and prune, or this repo grows a body mesh per keystroke.
