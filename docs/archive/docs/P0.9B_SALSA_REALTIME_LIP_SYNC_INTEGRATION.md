## Objective

Normal performer speech must automatically produce convincing realtime mouth articulation while retaining emotional intent:

```csharp
succubus.Say(clip);
await succubus.SayAsync(clip);
succubus.StopSpeaking();
```

Reuse the exact existing head-mounted `SpeechAudio` AudioSource referenced by `SuccubusPerformer.SpeechAudioSource`. It supplies both spatial voice playback and SALSA analysis. Do not create another AudioSource or move the existing source.

No preprocessing, phoneme asset, replacement speech API, or speech-queue redesign.

SALSA is amplitude-driven speech approximation, not phoneme recognition. Acceptance concerns believable articulation, timing, and emotional composition; it does not promise exact per-phoneme pronunciation. A future need for precise close-up pronunciation may require a different analysis approach, independently of facial mixing.

## Preserve P0.9A speech semantics

Preserve the existing public properties and methods:

```csharp
public AudioSource SpeechAudioSource { get; }
public bool IsSpeaking { get; }
public AudioClip CurrentSpeechClip { get; }
public int PendingSpeechCount { get; }
public void Say(AudioClip clip);
public Awaitable<SpeechCompletion> SayAsync(AudioClip clip);
public void StopSpeaking();
```

`PerformerSpeech` remains the sole playback and strict FIFO queue owner. Preserve Finished / Cancelled / PerformerDisabled completion, duplicate requests, and reentrant ordering.

Keep SALSA-specific types and cleanup out of `PerformerSpeech`. Do not introduce a lip-sync awaitable. Audio completion remains speech completion even if the mouth continues its short release animation. Silence inside a clip is not line completion.

Do not restart, reinitialize, or clear SALSA between immediate queued clips. Smooth articulation may continue across a handoff; a neutral frame between lines is not required.

## Facial ownership and composition policy

Begin with the concrete ownership model:

| System | Owned controls |
| --- | --- |
| Expression | Emotional morphs and eligible authored facial-bone position/rotation |
| SALSA | Exact dedicated speech blendshapes |
| Blink | Autonomous full-blink controls |
| Gaze / Attention Life | Head and eyes |
| Breathing | Existing breathing controls |

Preserve P0.8.1 facial-bone support beneath `upperFaceRig` and `lowerJaw`, including eligible jaw, lip, tongue, and teeth articulation. Continue excluding gaze-owned `head`, `lEye`, and `rEye`.

SALSA visemes must be Shape-only. Do not configure SALSA Bone controllers, EmoteR, or SALSA Eyes. SALSA is not another PlayableGraph layer in this ticket.

Disjoint properties prevent direct writer contention, but they do not guarantee a plausible combined deformation. Expression bones and speech morphs can affect the same mouth anatomy. Combined visual quality is a completion requirement, not an automatically deferred issue.

### Required graph ownership correction

At the reviewed baseline, `PerformerBodyPose` binds all blendshapes and `PerformerPoseStateJob` writes those weights. Snapshot, pose-sampling, and restore paths also read or write the full blendshape set. Excluding speech shapes from Expression alone therefore does not establish exclusive speech ownership.

Before claiming ownership correctness:

1. Audit every graph stage and snapshot/sample/restore path touching selected speech controls.
2. Prefer excluding speech-owned controls from BodyPose's effective writes, using the same exact speech catalog and preserving pose behavior for other channels.
3. If exclusion is materially unsuitable, a deliberately verified SALSA external-influence configuration and evaluation order is acceptable. Document the remaining writers and why their composition is stable; do not claim zero writer overlap.
4. Verify final rendered speech weights during settled poses, transitions, interrupted transitions, pose sampling, graph evaluation, and disable/re-enable.
5. Ensure a transient speech pose is not captured and later restored as the performer's neutral mouth state.

Do not resolve this by relying on incidental script order or a per-frame zeroing loop. Verify the installed QueueProcessor's external-influence behavior rather than assuming current online defaults match the package.

### Bounded response to visual conflicts

First tune selected speech shapes, maximum extents, and transition timing. Evaluate combinations before adding composition code.

If representative expressions still prevent convincing speech, this ticket permits a small performer-specific speech-aware reduction of conflicting lower-face Expression contributions:

- Affect only audited problematic jaw/lip/tongue/teeth channels or explicitly identified lower-face morphs.
- Preserve upper-face emotion and unrelated facial contributions.
- Apply the reduction where Expression contributions are evaluated, rather than adding another Transform writer.
- Keep the authored Expression asset and directing API unchanged; restore its full influence smoothly afterward.
- Use stable attack/release behavior across brief silence and FIFO handoffs. Do not toggle the whole Expression according to `IsSALSAing` each frame.
- Keep speech queue/completion semantics unchanged and describe the policy and tuning in the report.

This adjustment must be driven by demonstrated failures, not added speculatively. It does not remove facial-bone support from Expression.

Do not build shared-bone additive mixing, a generic facial arbiter, or a universal morph mixer. If convincing articulation requires speech bone movement that cannot be supplied by the exported shapes, stop at that checkpoint and report the concrete requirement for a focused follow-up. Do not quietly weaken acceptance or introduce direct SALSA bone writers.

Any future shared-bone design should have one final writer that composes Expression and speech contributions with character-specific constraints. Adding rotations and clamping individual axes alone is not a sufficient anatomical safety policy.

## Checkpoint 1 — actual Lara articulation audit

Enumerate blendshape names from Lara's actual current `SkinnedMeshRenderer` meshes. Do not guess DAZ names, assume a DAZ OneClick is installed, or invent bindings from memory.

Record all relevant candidates for phoneme/viseme, mouth opening/shape, and speech articulation. Prefer actual dedicated speech controls over Smile, Frown, Sneer, or other emotional controls.

For each proposed binding record:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Logical viseme | Human-readable configuration label |
| Imported name | Exact current blendshape name |
| Renderer | Exact path relative to a documented binding root |
| Index | Resolved current index, validated against the name |
| Source control | Exact DAZ control name where known; explicitly unknown otherwise |
| Rest and extent | Proposed minimum/rest and maximum useful weights, with units |
| Timing | On, hold if applicable, and off durations |

Preview every candidate at its proposed maximum and intermediate weights. Check lips, jaw opening, teeth, and tongue together, including front and three-quarter views. Verify it does not distort eyes or unrelated body areas and does not primarily express emotion.

An exported blendshape name alone does not prove that the complete DAZ articulation survived export. Confirm that the shape-only configuration produces the necessary visible motion, including oral geometry. Report missing articulation or export dependencies before broad implementation.

Start from SALSA's recommended sequence `w, f, t, th, ow, ee, oo`, but choose the best visually validated set for Lara. Seven existing names do not mandate seven configured visemes. Fewer useful shapes are preferable to misleading or poor configurations; report omissions and the reason.

The sequence represents progressively useful visual configurations selected from amplitude, not detected phonemes.

A logical viseme may contain multiple exact Shape components, including coordinated oral shapes on additional renderers if required. Do not impose one viseme = one blendshape. A shared shape appearing in multiple visemes is allowed only when intentional and supported by the installed QueueProcessor's behavior; test its transitions and record it once in the ownership catalog.

Do not guess among renderers with matching names. Resolve exact paths. Missing or ambiguous bindings must fail diagnostically.

- Rebuild only configurations it owns, preserving unrelated authored data.
- Create complete serialized helper and runtime controller data using the installed API.
- Use the intended initialization/baking path; verify any `configReady` / `Initialize()` requirements against installed examples.
- Configure trigger distribution after the ordered viseme list, using the supported API.
- Support Undo and mark the appropriate scene/prefab objects dirty and saveable.
- Remain idempotent after repeated execution and scene reload.

Do not depend on a DAZ-specific OneClick or downloadable proprietary asset absent from the repository.

Preserve AudioSource volume, pitch, mixer group, spatial blend, rolloff, distances, doppler, spatializer, and other authored playback settings. SALSA observes the source; it does not play or select clips.

Use normal AudioSource analysis with `useExternalAnalysis = false`. No microphone mode, external streaming analysis, or custom audio-analysis pipeline.

Begin with installed package/support-code recommendations. Starting guidance:

```text

loCutoff approximately 0.015–0.03
hiCutoff approximately 0.75

```

Tune visually rather than asserting exact values in tests. Record final dynamics settings, including jitter and secondary mixing if used. Allow reducing dynamics when a demonstrated artifact warrants it.

Verify shape weight units through the installed API; do not confuse a normalized OneClick amount with Unity blendshape weights. Derive useful extents from actual previews rather than universally assuming 100 is safe.

## Runtime integration and lifecycle

An optional narrow `PerformerSalsaLipSync` bridge may validate references, bindings, ownership, runtime readiness, diagnostics, and integration-specific lifecycle behavior.

Keep SALSA arrays/tuning out of `SuccubusPerformer`; minimize changes there. The bridge does not own playback, queueing, dialogue, gaze, Blink, or emotional directing.

Prefer normal SALSA release behavior after natural completion and `StopSpeaking`. Do not globally zero speech controls while idle. Explicit cleanup is permitted only where lifecycle evidence requires it and must affect only owned controls using installed supported semantics.

Test these distinct cases:

1. Disabling/re-enabling `SuccubusPerformer` while its GameObject stays active.
2. Disabling/re-enabling the SALSA integration/component.
3. Deactivating/reactivating the complete character GameObject.
4. Repeated cycles during speech, release animation, and silence.

Keep QueueProcessor enabled while the character remains active, following the package's intended lifecycle. Do not casually disable it to flush state. Whole-GameObject deactivation necessarily stops callbacks; explicitly handle owned-controller cleanup/restoration so reactivation cannot revive stale articulation. Verify behavior against the installed package.

Speech-owned controls return to their configured rest values. The combined face retains Expression and may intentionally remain open or asymmetric. Rest does not mean resetting the entire face to neutral.

Configuration failure must be diagnostic and disable affected lip-sync processing without breaking otherwise valid speech playback. Report missing references, invalid/mismatched bindings, duplicate components, and forbidden ownership clearly; avoid repeated log spam.
